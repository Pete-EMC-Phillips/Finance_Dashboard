🏦 Building your pension

Live DC pension — history & starting point

Your current active workplace or personal DC scheme. Log each actual valuation from your annual statement below to build a genuine track record — your balance today isn't just a static number, it's the result of contributions and investment growth over the years. The most recent entry becomes the starting point for every projection below.

Valuation history

One row per statement, in date order — usually annual, at the same point in your financial year. "Paid in this period" is what went in (you + employer + any lump sums) since the previous row; "Implied return" backs out the investment growth after removing that contribution (Simple Dietz method — assumes contributions land evenly through the period). The first row is your starting value, so it has no contribution or return figure. Use "Add a year" each time you get a new valuation, so this keeps building into a genuine long-run track record rather than a one-off snapshot.

Tax-free lump sum

UK pensions normally let you take up to 25% of your DC pot as a tax-free lump sum when you retire, up to the Lump Sum Allowance (£268,275 for 2026/27) — minus any tax-free cash already taken from the DB schemes above. Moving it into savings makes it accessible immediately, outside pension rules; the rest stays invested and is drawn down as pension.

💷 Spending it in retirement

Retirement income & State Pension

Model spending less as retirement progresses (e.g. once travel/big purchases slow down, then again in very old age). Leave an age at 0 to skip that step — each one only kicks in if your pots are projected to last that long.

Defaults to the full new State Pension for 2026/27 (£241.30/week, needs 35 qualifying NI years). Your actual entitlement may differ — check your personal forecast on gov.uk and adjust the figure above.

Retirement funding plan

Plan year by year, labelled by UK tax year (based on your date of birth and retirement age above) — e.g. spend some of your tax-free lump sum directly (no growth assumed while it waits) and move some into your ISA to grow, picking one source per year: your lump sum, savings, or an extra drawdown from your pension pot. ISA allowance is £20,000/yr. Money moved into your ISA this way stays untouched unless the Drawdown allocation table below gives that year's ISA % above 0 — new years default to 0% ISA, so it's protected and free to grow by default. Applies to the medium growth scenario only; low/high still follow the same drawdown allocation table. Add one row per year of retirement, in order — leave both boxes at 0 to skip a year.

📊 Drawdown & cash flow

Drawdown allocation

Set exactly where each year's income shortfall (beyond DB/State pension and any funding-plan spend) comes from — split across your pension pot, savings, ISA and unallocated tax-free lump sum, as a % per year. Savings % only draws on your plain Savings balance from section 2 — if that's £0 (e.g. because your tax-free lump sum is being routed via the funding plan's "move into ISA" instead), Savings % has nothing to draw and the shortfall falls to whichever other bucket you've weighted. If your spare cash lives in your ISA, weight ISA % instead; if it's still sitting as unspent tax-free cash (not yet allocated by the funding plan, or left over once its rows run out), weight Lump sum % to spend it directly — it doesn't grow while unspent, so this is the only way to draw it down without it sitting idle forever. A bucket set to 0% for a year is never touched that year, even if the others run dry. Applies to all three growth scenarios (low/high only have a lump sum to spend if you've taken a tax-free lump sum, since they don't use the funding plan); rows cover every year from retirement to the age you're planning to. Use "Fill all years" to set a default split quickly, then tweak individual years below.

Illustrative only — not financial advice. Assumes constant monthly compounding, a flat salary that only changes when you add a scheduled pay rise, and steady contribution rates between scheduled changes. Dormant DC pots compound from their valuation date at the same low/medium/high rates as your live scheme, with no further contributions. The tax-free lump sum option assumes you take it as a single withdrawal at retirement, capped by the UK Lump Sum Allowance (£268,275 for 2026/27) less any tax-free cash you record as already taken from DB schemes — it does not check scheme-specific commutation limits, doesn't model the Money Purchase Annual Allowance (MPAA) that taking taxable pension income can trigger, and ignores the separate £1,073,100 Lump Sum and Death Benefit Allowance. Savings and ISA are modelled as two separate pots, each growing at the same three rates. In retirement, each year's income shortfall (beyond DB/State pension, other income, and any funding-plan tax-free spend) is split between your pension pot, savings, ISA and unallocated tax-free lump sum according to the % you set for that year in the Drawdown allocation table — a bucket set to 0% for a year is never drawn on that year even if the others run out, in which case the shortfall counts towards that scenario "running out"; this table applies to all three growth scenarios, though low/high only have a lump sum to draw on if you've taken one (they don't use the funding plan). DB pensions and the State Pension are summed and assumed flat in cash terms. The UK pension Annual Allowance is currently £60,000/tax year (2026/27) across all your pensions combined; this tool checks your live scheme's rolling 12-month employee + employer contributions against it as a rough guide only — it does not account for the tapered allowance, MPAA, or carry forward, and doesn't include savings/ISA contributions since those aren't pension contributions. If you've added rows to the retirement funding plan, they apply on top of the drawdown allocation table for the medium scenario only, in year order starting from your retirement date; any tax-free cash left unallocated (or leftover once your rows run out) earns 0% while unspent and is only drawn down via the "Lump sum %" column — it's never auto-swept elsewhere. The cash journey chart assumes withdrawals are taken at the start of each year, remaining balances keep growing at their scenario's rate, and State/DB/other income only counts from the ages you set. Your "reduce income" steps apply in order of age, each overriding the last once reached. It all ignores tax on income drawn (beyond the tax-free lump sum), ISA tax-free status aside, inflation, and State Pension age changes.
